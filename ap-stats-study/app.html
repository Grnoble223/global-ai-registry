2A

Two categorical variables

The main question

For almost every two-way table problem, ask: what should the denominator be?

Marginal

Uses a margin total.

Denominator: grand total

"of all…"

Joint

Two categories together.

Denominator: grand total

"…AND…"

Conditional

Only one group.

Denominator: that group's total

"GIVEN / among / of the…"

Compare groups

Use percentages, not counts, when group sizes differ.

Association

If conditional percentages differ across groups, there is an association. Association does not prove causation.

Graphs

Segmented bar chart: compares conditional percentages. Mosaic plot: also shows group sizes with different bar widths. No association → the segments line up about the same.

2B

Randomness, probability & simulation

Random process

The result is uncertain. Outcome = one result. Event = one or more outcomes.

Probability

The long-run relative frequency of an event over many repetitions.

Law of large numbers

More repetitions → the observed proportion gets closer to the true probability. Short runs can look messy.

Randomness myth

Random doesn't mean perfectly alternating. Streaks happen. Past independent results don't make an outcome "due."

Simulation, 4 steps

1 State the probability. 2 Plan the random model. 3 Do many trials. 4 Conclude: successes ÷ trials.

2C

Probability rules

Sample space

All possible outcomes.

Probability model

Outcomes + probabilities. Each is between 0 and 1, and they all add to 1.

Formulas

Equally likely: P(event) = favorable ÷ total outcomes
NOT: P(Aᶜ) = 1 − P(A)
AT LEAST ONE: 1 − P(none)
OR: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)

Why subtract?

The overlap got counted twice. If events are mutually exclusive, the overlap is 0, so just add.

Symbols

A ∩ B = A AND B (the overlap). A ∪ B = A OR B (either or both).

2D

Conditional probability & independence

Formulas

GIVEN: P(A | B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B)
AND: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B | A)
If independent: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)

Denominator trick

Whatever comes after the | is your new group, so it's your denominator.

Check independence

Compare P(A | B) with P(A). If they're equal, knowing B doesn't change A, so the events are independent.

Mutually exclusive

Can't happen together. P(A and B) = 0.

Independent

One doesn't change the other. P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).

Tree diagrams

Use for multiple stages. Multiply along a path. Add the complete paths that fit the event. Branches leaving the same point add to 1.
